open_ext4 for Mac — First-Run Guide
===================================

A native ext2/ext3/ext4 driver for macOS, built on FSKit. Three steps and
your Linux disks read and write like any other volume.


1. Install the app
------------------

Drag Ext4Mac.app onto the Applications folder in this window.


2. Open Ext4Mac once
--------------------

This step is not optional, and it is the one people skip.

macOS registers a file-system extension when its containing app RUNS —
not when you install it. Until Ext4Mac has been opened at least once, the
extension does not appear in System Settings at all. Not switched off:
absent. So if you go looking for it before opening the app, you will find
an empty list and conclude the installer is broken.

Open it from Applications. It lives in the menu bar and will ask you for
whatever is still missing.


3. Approve the extension
------------------------

macOS requires a person to approve a file-system extension. No app can do
it for you — an app that could would be a hole in the sandbox — which is
why nothing prompts on its own.

Ext4Mac offers to open the right pane for you. Or go there directly:

  System Settings  →  General  →  Login Items & Extensions
    →  File System Extensions  →  turn on "open_ext4 (ext2/3/4)"

Plug in an ext4 disk (or open a disk image) and it mounts read-write
immediately.


Keep "Start at Login" turned on
-------------------------------

Ext4Mac offers this on first run, and it is worth accepting. The
registration from step 2 does not survive a restart: after a reboot, the
extension is missing again until something opens the app. Starting at
login does that for you, so the approval you gave in step 3 keeps working.

If you decline and later change your mind, the menu-bar menu has the
toggle, or run:

    /Applications/Ext4Mac.app/Contents/MacOS/Ext4Mac login-item on


Encrypted (LUKS) volumes
------------------------

Plug one in and Ext4Mac asks for the passphrase, then mounts it under its
own name. The passphrase is typed into the app and never reaches the
sandboxed extension, which only ever sees a derived key.

    Ext4Mac list            which volumes are unlocked
    Ext4Mac forget <uuid>   forget a volume's key again


Formatting from Disk Utility (optional)
---------------------------------------

    sudo make install-diskutil

adds ext2/ext3/ext4 to Disk Utility's Erase menu and to
`diskutil listFilesystems`.


Notes
-----

- Requires macOS 15.4 or later on Apple Silicon.
- Eject removable media before unplugging it.
- Keep a backup of anything you cannot lose. This is young software.

Project and source:  https://github.com/h3ct0r/open_ext4_for_mac
